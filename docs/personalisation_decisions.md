# RootedFit Personalisation Decisions

## Food discovery and choice autonomy

RootedFit now begins with a **Nigeria-first set of 50 foods and ingredients**, with equivalent editable starter catalogues for Ghana, Kenya, South Africa, the United Kingdom, the United States, and a global fallback. This catalogue is a discovery aid, not a procurement promise: users choose only items that are genuinely available to them. The product keeps a separate favourite-fruit set, which lets fruit appear as an optional snack, drink, or grocery suggestion without treating it as compulsory.

Dietary restrictions are treated as hard exclusions; disliked foods are treated as soft exclusions; favourite meals remain plan anchors. This distinction protects autonomy and reduces the likelihood that the app removes a user’s familiar comfort foods without a clear reason.

## Specificity without medical claims

The reference materials use a valuable structure—day labels, meal timing, concrete items, preparation steps, and market categories. RootedFit now adopts that practical structure while avoiding claims that a food, drink, or workout can target fat loss, change a specific body area, or treat postpartum physiology. The planner is a non-clinical habit and meal-organisation tool; it should route condition-specific, pregnancy, or postpartum content through clinician-reviewed pathways before launch.

## Measurement and photo privacy

Metric values are stored as canonical centimetres and kilograms, while the onboarding and tracker can display inches and pounds. Weekly measurement records include weight, waist, hip, chest, upper arm, and thigh. Progress photos use explicit front, side, and back slots. In the MVP, selected photos are copied into the app’s private on-device documents area and their URIs are persisted locally; they are neither uploaded nor shared.

## Next content-engine requirement

Country catalogues should mature into reviewed regional data packs rather than a single worldwide list. Each pack should include common foods, seasonal substitutions, staple preparation methods, food-safety storage notes, and a local recipe library. FAO’s Western Africa food-composition resource and country food-based dietary guidance are appropriate starting sources for governance of the Nigeria-first catalogue. [1] [2]
